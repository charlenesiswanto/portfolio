Why Research Comparison Identity Decisions Result
01

Indonesia has 17,001 islands. Bali gets nearly all the attention.

Lombok sits just east of Bali, with its own culture, landmarks and landscapes, and it is still overshadowed by its neighbour. A large part of the gap is online presence: there is little to find, so there is little reason to go.

As a local Indonesian I hear about places like Lombok through word of mouth. A tourist relying on an internet search never gets that conversation. That was the starting point: give an overlooked beach town the online presence it is missing, as the centrepiece of a wider rebrand.

03

I interviewed travellers before I drew anything.

They came from different backgrounds and had different travel experience. I chose interviews over a survey because I wanted detail on behaviour and pain points, not only counts.

They wantPlaces near me, right nowDining, attractions and hotels based on their real-time location.
They struggle withComplex navigationPlus long loading times and information that turns out to be incomplete.
They valueSimple and completeStraightforward features, one-click access to full details, up-to-date information.
They respond toReal rewardsPoints only matter when they turn into discounts or cashback.
They needReal-time updatesNotifications matter most during peak travel seasons.
They choose byWhat friends usePeer popularity, referral codes and vouchers. Traveloka was the app mentioned most.
04

The studies agree: price leads, photos decide, and people scan.

Price leadsPrice plays a vital role in travel website use, and people show a clear preference for lower-priced items (Bing Pan, 2011).
Photos decidePeople engage most with property photos and base decisions on visual appeal, even paying more for places with attractive photos (Bing Pan, 2011).
People scanScanning is the main way people process information online, so content has to be concise and visual (Astleitner & Leutner, 1995).
05

I compared four travel sites. None let a visitor learn about a place, trust the reviews and book it without leaving.

I went through each platform and its Google Play reviews, and sorted what I found into what to borrow and what to fix.

Traveloka

Borrowed
Categories that put discounts and rewards up front, nearby attractions, destination articles, a top bar for navigation.
Fixed
Information overload and sections opening in new tabs. Reviews ask for human support when the chatbot cannot answer.

Visit Singapore

Borrowed
Minimal design, clear type, traveller essentials such as documents and transport, a chatbot for real-time help.
Fixed
No direct booking, no visitor reviews, and information that does not adjust to current location and time.

Tripadvisor

Borrowed
Credible reviews, a personalised itinerary and an interactive map.
Fixed
Bookings redirect to third parties, the volume of content overwhelms, and culture and history get little space.

Visit Finland

Borrowed
Fun facts through the interface, local weather and time, filtering activities by preference.
Fixed
No bookings, no user reviews, and trouble saving favourite locations.
06

So Aruna puts every part of a trip on one platform.

Planning, booking, preparing and the travel itself.

Personalised planningSet budget, activities and group size, get an itinerary that can still be edited.
Interactive mapExplore the town's attractions and amenities visually.
One searchHotels, transport, food and amenities, with photos, prices, ratings and reviews.
Help on handA free AI chatbot, and a way to connect with locals by chat or in person.
PreparationRequired documents, packing and transport guidance for the dates chosen.
07

Three pages follow the three stages of a trip: get curious, book, commit.

Interviewees complained about complex navigation, so I sorted every feature into a flow before drawing a screen.

Discover ArunaWelcome, slogan and fun fact, featured destinations, history and culture, articles and local news, quick search.
Things To DoDirect booking for stays, tickets, restaurants and rentals, with search, filters and recommendations.
Plan Your TripA short questionnaire that produces a customisable itinerary, recommendations and a map.
08

The name means the glow of sunrise. The colours come from Lombok batik.

"Aruna" comes from Sanskrit: the reddish glow of the rising sun. It stands for new beginnings, and it gave the slogan, Where Every Sunrise is a New Adventure.

Iterating the logo

I tried the name in several script typefaces. All were elegant, but Playball kept the best balance of elegance and readability, so the mark stays recognisable. Floral elements add natural beauty and a sense of renewal.

09

Each screen answers something a person said, a study found or a competitor got wrong.

One view to orient

People scan, so the first screen holds three things: the slogan, one random fun fact about the town (an idea from Visit Finland) and a search bar for quick access.

Weather and time, borrowed

Visit Finland shows local weather and time. Aruna does the same on its homepage, top: their widget, bottom: mine, so visitors know the conditions before they plan.

Deals first, booking in place

Discounts are the first thing people notice on a travel site, so Best Deals leads the page, with large photos because photos drive decisions. Cards carry reviews for credibility, a Book Now button and a favourite, which closes the gap the tourism-board sites left open.

Near me, filtered

Interviewees wanted places based on where they are. The page opens on the most searched categories, a search bar and a filter, plus "Explore activities in your location" for nearby places and essentials such as supermarkets, laundry and gas stations.

A plan in a few questions

Inspired by Tripadvisor's itinerary feature. Visitors answer a short questionnaire on dates, budget, group size, activities and accommodation, and get a day-by-day itinerary with required documents, weather and places to stay. Saved favourites can be added in.

A map, and a human

A 3D interactive map lets visitors click any location for details, photos and reviews. An AI chatbot answers questions in real time, and because reviews of other apps asked for human support, every footer carries a hotline and a feedback link.
